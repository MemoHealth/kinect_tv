# Playable worlds — feasibility and delivery proposal

Planning proposal, 2026-11-10. Future IDs are provisional. This document records source inspection and existing local evidence. Third-party applications and the proposed worlds were not executed.

## Starting point

Magic Rain is already a local desktop application with Python, NumPy and pygame-ce/SDL2. `sensors/kinect_v1.py` uses the system-installed Microsoft SDK 1.8. The capture worker supplies the latest depth frame. `live.py` and `vision/` handle calibration, filtering and silhouette recovery. `Viewport`, `Outline`, and `Presenter` share the path from the 320 × 240 mask to the 1280 × 720 logical canvas and onward to the TV. `MotionField` derives motion on a 40 × 30 grid. `LightPaint` already keeps a sensor-sized trail with a three-second half-life. `ColorMirror` caches its remap. The rain loop already uses fixed simulation steps and bounded particles. Scene switching is currently organized in `app.py`. These are the components to extend. [Architecture](../01_Architecture/system-and-modules.md) · [Current scene behavior](../02_Experience/scenes-and-progressive-enhancement.md)

On the target Windows 10 machine, previous tests measured about 60.6 FPS with live depth on a virtual display, and 295 depth frames and 259 registered color/depth pairs in a separate 10-second SDK probe. A 14-second TCL test averaged 27.1 FPS while the Kinect was busy. Read-only display enumeration confirms the TCL runs at 3840 × 2160 and 59 Hz on Intel Iris Plus Graphics 640 alongside an Intel Core i7-7560U. This verifies output geometry and identifies a performance risk. Physical three-person play, real HDMI latency and sustained performance remain open. [Manual checks](../../docs/manual-live-checklist.md) · [Performance targets](../01_Architecture/performance.md)

## Shared design

Use one capture owner and one calibrated silhouette for every world. A scene receives the existing mirrored mask and, where useful, contour or coarse motion values plus the existing simulation timestep. Keep coordinates and mirroring in the shared path. Keep masks and frames in memory. Scenes receive derived data as read-only and keep only their own bounded animation state. Capture, camera setup, settings, menu, presentation and shutdown stay in the app. [Current frame and foreground contracts](../01_Architecture/contracts-and-algorithms.md)

During Paint Explosion integration, extract an update/draw boundary only where Paint duplicates the lifecycle of Rain or an existing scene. Reset a world when its trails or particles should clear. Only one world updates and renders at a time. Share the existing mask edges, cached sprites and geometry.

Each visible silhouette contributes locally, including three participants with RGB and pose tracking absent. Where two people overlap, the merged visible mask is valid and the obscured body has no visible response until it reappears. Kinect v1 provides up to six skeleton slots with full joint tracking for up to two active people. Optional registered color can repair small edge gaps while the depth-first path remains functional. Galaxy Mirror uses a recognizable star silhouette. Any later request to show the actual RGB image in that world is a separate adult-controlled choice and can reuse the existing color mirror. [Microsoft Kinect v1 skeletal tracking overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/msdn-magazine/2012/april/kinect-context-aware-dialogue-with-kinect)

Where a world needs distinct colors, derive up to three local color slots from coherent regions in the existing foreground mask. Compare overlap and centroid proximity with the prior depth frame. Use a short hold through momentary loss, a shared or blended palette while regions merge, and recover previous slots on a split when spatial evidence is unambiguous. Assignment stays stable while players are visually separable. After full occlusion or departure, offer a clean new slot. Slots are transient scene state, with no biometric matching, player profiles or saved identifiers. Rain and Aquarium can consume the same visible mask without color assignment.

### Graphics and interaction

| World | What movement does | Proposed rendering path |
|---|---|---|
| **Magic Rain** | A stationary body redirects rain. A sweep pushes droplets. Cupped arms can hold water in the current scene. | Preserve existing rain, contour, cached streaks and arm-water simulation. Use it as the baseline for visual and performance regressions. |
| **Paint Explosion** | Ordinary movement paints. Fast movements such as dancing, quick steps or an air punch can make short bursts. The world gives continuous feedback for gentle movement and never prompts speed or impact. | Reuse `LightPaint`’s edge sampling and fading history. Emit fixed-capacity 2D spray from changed edge regions. Scale the local emission rate smoothly with movement and cap luminance. Give up to three separable participants different colors. |
| **Galaxy Mirror** | A living galaxy traces the body while it rests. Arm movement scatters nearby stars, which drift and fade. | Reuse the mirrored mask, outline and fade buffers. Use cached star sprites, a few shallow parallax layers and a bounded particle mode. Apply forces locally near the moving contour rather than animating all stars on every movement. |
| **Living Aquarium** | Fish approach upper outer points of nearby silhouettes and drift back after the body leaves. Jellyfish slowly avoid silhouettes. | The implemented baseline approximates hand locations from the upper outer contour. Cached sprites and bounded steering operate without joint tracking. Confidence-gated hand motion and a broader motion fallback remain future refinements. |

Keep simulation on CPU, vectorize work in NumPy and allocate buffers at scene startup or resize. Draw the logical canvas once. Preserve the TV’s physical output using the existing SDL path. On the Intel iGPU, profile before tuning. Reduce optional sprite counts, glow and trail detail, then effect-layer resolution, keeping capture rate and alignment. Consider lower logical resolution only after profiling confirms a presentation bottleneck and visual checks show readable contours. No dedicated GPU, new framework or native dependency is part of the baseline.

Children start by entering the camera field. Resting and moving both create feedback. The active world stays fixed until an adult changes it from the existing panel. Normal play has no instructions, HUD, scores or mandatory gestures. The adult retains camera controls, mute and mild settings. Where a world uses trails, add readable fade options such as `Kort`, `Normal` and `Lång` with explicit proposed half-lives of 1.5, 3 and 6 seconds. Expose a small set of configurable palettes with up to three distinguishable player colors and retain the current Ljusmåleri, Snö and Lustiga huset defaults during settings migration. [Parent panel and play rules](../02_Experience/interaction-and-menu.md)

### Optional Paint Explosion artwork export

The adult panel can hold and preview the current artwork before it fades and explicitly save a flattened local PNG. The export surface contains generated paint and trail layers only. Keep the composed game screen, live RGB, depth arrays, mask buffers, player slots, debug metrics and metadata outside the export path. Generated strokes can still suggest a body shape, so present the preview and require an intentional adult action for each file. Returning to play discards the temporary preview. There is no background autosave or transmission. [Privacy requirements](../03_Quality/privacy-and-safety.md)

## Smoother contours and Dansspegel

**MR-26 — Existing outline refinement.** Schedule after the baseline check and before relying on fine contours in the new worlds. The current renderer smoothly scales coverage from the 320 × 240 mask and looks up edge alpha. Compare that baseline with a small, render-only coverage filter. Cache the result per new mask and preserve the width of thin arms and wrists. Weather, particles and arm-water collision continue to use the original mask. Scope this issue to `render/outline.py` and its synthetic fixtures.

**Acceptance.** Compare still and moving horizontal, diagonal and curved contours on 1080p and the TCL at 4K. Look for fewer visible steps, legible thin limbs and responsive arm sweeps without a lingering duplicate contour. Keep the gaps between arms and torso and the opening of an arm-shaped cup. Verify identical collision results for the same input and meet the shared performance gate. Applies to MR-UX-04, MR-ART-01/05/06 and MR-SYS-04.

**MR-27 — Dansspegel.** Astra proposes a calm midnight-blue scene (`#091321`) with rounded strokes, a round head and brighter hands and feet. Start with 7–10 logical pixels of stroke width at 1280 × 720. Use the planned palette controls for turquoise (`#57D9D0`), coral (`#FFAA91`) and lavender (`#B9A7F4`) accents. Round the line ends and connect them at the joints. If local antialiasing needs more coverage, draw the tightly cropped figure layer at double resolution and smoothscale it back. Keep hand trails short and adult-adjustable.

Start with a silhouette that mirrors movement for all visible players. Gate articulated limbs on a separate pose feasibility check after the baseline. If that check passes without a driver change or a regression in capture, pass fresh, confident joints through the existing shared viewport. Use stronger smoothing when still and follow quick moves more closely. Try a 50–90 ms smoothing time constant and tune it against measured latency. Fade unreliable limbs into the matching silhouette without extending stale joints indefinitely. Kinect v1 supports up to two full joint tracks. A third visible player still receives a moving silhouette. Applies to MR-UX-02–04/06, MR-CV-02–04, MR-ART-03–06 and MR-SYS-04.

**Acceptance.** Use synthetic joints to check quiet poses, crossed arms, quick direction changes, mirror direction and gradual loss. On the target Kinect and TCL, confirm that hands and feet read from the normal viewing distance, two confident poses can coexist if supported, and all three people keep a visible response without RGB or reliable joints. Check responsiveness and the shared frame-rate floor. Treat the proposed friendly skeleton look as a later issue after the stick figure works. Reuse the same joints with rounded bones and a stylized rib cage. The skeleton look has no separate tracking requirement.

## Risks and decisions to defer

- **Clipping and occlusion.** A far background can be black in the depth preview, and a limb outside the sensor field has no measurement. Validate the MR-18 depth fallback and the adult framing guidance before testing added effects. Never infer an identity from a merged contour.
- **USB bandwidth and depth/color alignment.** Start and test each core world with depth alone. If using existing RGB enhancements, check actual synchronized pair availability, mask quality, latency and the transition to depth-only when color drops. Preserve the installed Kinect driver and existing integration.
- **4K presentation cost and comfort.** The measured short HDMI run missed the render target. Profile on the real display before increasing graphical load, report the actual mode and frame rate, and reduce optional effects if needed. Cap brightness and emission, avoid rapid luminance changes, and let the adult mute audio.
- **Scope and licenses.** First-party code and simple procedural assets are the default. New packages, code from GPL or unlicensed sources, Unity/OBS/OpenGL compute, Kinect Fusion, face tracking, and saved camera imagery are outside these slices. A future candidate needs a distinct issue and evidence of a concrete benefit.
